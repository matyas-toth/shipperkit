Kamal and Shipper solve the same problem from opposite ends. Kamal is a deploy tool. It runs where you are, connects to your servers over SSH and leaves nothing behind except containers and its proxy. Shipper is a platform. It lives on the server and keeps track of applications, databases, domains, backups and the people who can change them.

## Choose ShipperKit if

- **You want databases looked after.** Shipper creates them, gives you a connection string, rotates credentials and backs them up on a schedule. In Kamal, a database is an accessory you run and protect yourself.
- **More than one person deploys.** Teammates sign in with their own accounts and roles instead of sharing SSH keys and registry credentials.
- **You'd rather not run a registry.** Shipper builds on the server, so a push to your branch deploys without pushing images anywhere.
- **You host more than your own app.** The Service Library deploys n8n, Grafana, Uptime Kuma and 360 more services with their secrets generated.
- **You want to see what is running** in a dashboard with logs, a terminal and every release.

## Choose Kamal if

- **You deploy one app to several servers** behind a load balancer.
- **You want configuration in the repository** and nothing extra running on the server.
- **You're on Rails**, where Kamal is the default and the community knows it well.

## Moving from Kamal

Your Dockerfile works in Shipper unchanged. Create an application from the same repository, copy the environment from your `.env` or secrets into Shipper's environment editor, restore your database dump as a new managed database, and switch DNS when it checks out. The [move an existing app](/docs/move-an-app/) guide walks through the order.
